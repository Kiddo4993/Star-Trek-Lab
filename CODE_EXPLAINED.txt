STAR TREK LAB: HOW THE CODE WORKS
=================================

A red-matter probe is dropped 2.00e6 m above a hole drilled to the centre
of Vulcan. The site works out how long it takes to get to the centre
(1.36e3 s) and how many Vulcans per second that is (5.15e6). It does this
with Euler's method, then draws the graphs and the animation.


1. WHERE EVERYTHING IS
----------------------

  src/lib/physics.ts        All the physics. No React, no imports.
  src/lib/format.ts         Rounds numbers to 3 significant figures.
  src/lib/theme.ts          Pen colours for the graphs.
  src/lib/fonts.ts          The handwriting font (Kalam).
  src/lib/timeline.ts       Keeps track of where we are in the fall.

  src/app/page.tsx          The page. It just shows <Lab />.
  src/app/layout.tsx        The <html> wrapper: title, font.
  src/app/globals.css       All the styling (graph paper, tape, pens).
  src/app/icon.svg          The browser-tab icon.

  src/components/Lab.tsx           The whole page: inputs, results, graphs.
  src/components/CrossSection.tsx  The animated drawing of Vulcan.
  src/components/Charts.tsx        One graph component that all the graphs use.
  src/components/Scrub.tsx         A number in a sentence you can drag.

  scripts/verify.mjs        Checks the answers are 1.36e3 s and 5.15e6.
  next.config.ts            Makes the site a plain static website.
  .github/workflows/deploy.yml   Publishes to GitHub Pages on every push.

physics.ts doesn't know anything about the website, and the website
doesn't do any physics itself. It calls physics.ts and draws what comes
back. Because of that, verify.mjs can check the physics without a browser.


2. THE PHYSICS (src/lib/physics.ts)
-----------------------------------

Signs (same everywhere):
  r = distance from Vulcan's centre (m)
  v = speed TOWARD the centre (m/s), positive while falling in
  a = acceleration TOWARD the centre (m/s^2)

v points inward, so r gets smaller as the probe falls. That's why the
position update is r = r - v*dt and not r = r + v*dt.

--- Constants ---
  G0 = 13.73, R = 6.792e6 and POPULATION = 7.00e9 are from the question.
  GM comes from those: at the surface g0 = GM/R^2, so GM = g0 * R^2.
  DT = 0.01 s is the Euler time step.

  The drag numbers (mass 2000 kg, Cd 0.47, air 1.2*e^(-h/8500)) are from
  the question too. PROBE_RADIUS = 0.5 m is my guess, because the question
  gives the mass but not the size, and drag needs the area A = pi*r^2.

--- gravity(r, interior) ---
  Outside (r >= R):   g = GM / r^2        Newton's law of gravity
  Inside, uniform:    g = G0 * r / R      only the mass closer to the
                                          centre than r pulls on it, and
                                          there's less of it the deeper
                                          you go, so g drops to 0
  Inside, dense core: g = G0              all the mass is in the middle,
                                          so g stays at 13.73

--- airDensity(h) ---
  h is the height above the surface (negative means in the shaft).
  Above ground it's 1.2 * e^(-h/8500).
  Inside the shaft is still a TODO FOR YOU. If you keep using the same
  formula underground you get e^(+799) at the centre, which is Infinity
  on a computer. For now it just uses sea-level air (1.2).

--- dragAccel(r, v) and netAccel(r, v, interior, drag) ---
  Drag force F = 1/2 * rho * Cd * A * v^2. Dividing by the mass turns it
  into an acceleration. Drag always pushes against the motion, so
      net acceleration = gravity - sign(v) * drag
  With drag off it's just gravity.

--- simulate(options): EULER'S METHOD ---
  The problem is that a keeps changing as the probe falls, so you can't
  use the constant-acceleration equations for the whole trip. Euler's
  method cuts the fall into tiny steps of dt = 0.01 s. In that short a
  time a barely changes, so for one step you can pretend it's constant.

  Start: r = R + dropHeight, v = launchSpeed, step = 0.
  Loop while r > 0:
      a = netAccel(r, v)      1. acceleration from where it is now
      v = v + a*dt            2. new velocity
      r = r - v*dt            3. move, using the NEW velocity
      step = step + 1
  Total time = step * dt. Doing the multiplication once at the end,
  instead of adding 0.01 thousands of times, stops rounding errors from
  building up.

  Step 3 uses the new v instead of the old one. That version is called
  Euler-Cromer. Inside the planet the probe moves like a mass on a spring,
  and plain Euler (using the old v) slowly adds energy that shouldn't be
  there. Euler-Cromer doesn't have that problem.

  How do you know dt is small enough? Run it again with a bigger dt. If
  the answer is the same, the step size isn't affecting it anymore. The
  page does this with dt = 1, 0.1 and 0.01 s, and verify.mjs checks that
  0.1 and 0.01 give the same answer to 3 s.f.

  While it loops it also:
    - saves the moment r first drops below R (crossing the surface) and
      the speed then
    - saves a "sample" {t, r, v, a, x} every 1 s of simulated time for the
      graphs and animation (x = distance fallen = r0 - r)
    - gives up after 200 000 s so a weird setting can't freeze the page

  It returns the samples plus totalTime, surfaceTime, surfaceSpeed,
  finalSpeed and rate = POPULATION / totalTime.

--- firstSteps(options, n) ---
  Gives the first 3 steps for the table in the Method section. It runs the
  real simulate() and saves every single step, so the table shows the same
  numbers the answer comes from. The a used in step k is the one worked
  out at the end of step k-1, which is why it reads s[k - 1].a.

--- rateVsHeight(...) ---
  Runs the whole simulation 41 times (0, 250, 500 ... 10 000 km) for the
  rate vs height graph. It uses dt = 0.05 s because that's 41 full runs,
  and the answer only changes by less than 0.1 %.

--- thin(list, max) ---
  A fall can have thousands of samples, but a graph only needs about 600
  points. This keeps every k-th one, plus the last one.


3. ROUNDING (src/lib/format.ts)
-------------------------------
  sf3(x): x.toPrecision(3) gives "1.36e+3", and this turns it into
          "1.36 × 10³". Every number on the page goes through it.
  tick(x): short axis labels like 12k or 5M.


4. THE PAGE (src/components/Lab.tsx)
------------------------------------
  State: four useState values, one per control (heightKm, launchSpeed,
  interior, drag).

  useDeferredValue: dragging a slider changes the value a lot of times a
  second, and every change re-runs several simulations. Deferring lets the
  slider keep moving smoothly while the maths catches up. "working it
  out…" shows up while it's behind.

  useMemo: each calculation only re-runs when its own inputs change.
    sim         the main Euler run (dt = 0.01 s)
    runs        the same drop with dt = 1 s and 0.1 s, for the results table
    steps       the first 3 Euler steps, for the Method section
    plotData    sim samples thinned out for the graphs
    comparison  four more runs: uniform/dense core, drag off/on
    gSeries     g at 301 distances, for both interior models
    rateData    the 41-height sweep

  The page goes top to bottom like a lab write-up:
    title block, Aim (the answer, with a red tick if dt = 0.1 s gives the
    same answer), Set-up (cross-section and controls), Method (how Euler's
    method works plus the first 3 steps), Results (table for each dt),
    Graphs, Uniform vs dense core, Working.

  The handwritten notes under some graphs (velocityNote, accelNote) only
  show up when they're true for the current settings. With drag on they're
  hidden, because what drag does depends on what you pick for airDensity.


5. THE ANIMATION (src/components/CrossSection.tsx)
--------------------------------------------------
  simTime is how far into the fall we are, in seconds of simulated time.

  While it's playing, requestAnimationFrame calls a function about 60
  times a second. Each time it checks how much real time went by and adds
      real seconds × playback speed (like 100×)
  to simTime, and it stops at the end.

  sampleAt(samples, t) finds the two saved samples on either side of t
  (binary search) and blends between them (linear interpolation). The
  samples are 1 s apart, but this way the probe still moves smoothly.

  Drawing: one scale factor turns metres into SVG pixels, picked so the
  release point and the bottom of the planet both fit. yOf(r) turns a
  distance from the centre into a y position. The depth ruler on the right
  uses the same scale, so the probe and its arrow on the ruler always line
  up. The ruler spacing (500, 1000, 2000 ... km) is picked so there are
  about 8 labels at most.

  The scrubber slider sets simTime directly and pauses. Changing any input
  resets the animation to t = 0.


6. THE GRAPHS (src/components/Charts.tsx)
-----------------------------------------
  All eight graphs use the same <Chart> component, which wraps Recharts:
    LineChart + XAxis/YAxis   axes with labels and units
    Line (one per series)     each series has its own data array, so lines
                              that end at different times share one x-axis
    ReferenceLine (marker)    red "surface" line at the crossing time
    ReferenceArea (rock)      pencil shading for "inside the rock"
    ReferenceDot (dot)        red circle for "this drop" on the rate graph
    Tooltip                   values when you hover, rounded to 3 s.f.

  Each graph looks like a printout taped into the notebook: handwritten
  title, white paper with tape (CSS .taped), printer-style axes, and notes
  in red pen. tilt rotates each one by less than a degree so they don't
  all sit perfectly straight.


7. LOOK AND FEEL (globals.css, theme.ts, fonts.ts)
--------------------------------------------------
  The page is supposed to look like a lab notebook:
    - green engineering paper, made from two sets of CSS gradient lines
      (20 px squares with a darker line every 100 px)
    - Kalam handwriting font, bundled at build time by next/font so it
      works on a static site
    - blue pen for the uniform model, green for the dense core, and red
      only for the probe and the surface line
    - yellow highlighter on the answers
    - buttons drawn as wobbly boxes (uneven border-radius)
  theme.ts has the same colours as plain hex codes, because SVG
  attributes in the graphs can't read CSS variables.


8. BUILDING AND DEPLOYING
-------------------------
  next.config.ts: output: "export" makes `npm run build` produce a plain
  folder of HTML/JS/CSS in ./out, so no server is needed. GitHub Pages
  puts the site at username.github.io/REPO-NAME/, so it needs a basePath.
  The workflow sets it, and on Vercel it stays empty.

  deploy.yml: on every push to main, GitHub installs the packages, runs
  `npm run verify` (and stops if the answers are wrong), builds, and
  publishes ./out to GitHub Pages.

  Commands:
    npm run dev      site at http://localhost:3000 that reloads on save
    npm run verify   PASS/FAIL for 1.36e3 s, 5.15e6 Vulcans/s and dt = 0.1 s
    npm run build    builds the static site into ./out


9. THE INTERACTIONS
-------------------
  Dragging numbers (Scrub.tsx). You can drag the drop height and launch
    speed in the Aim sentence sideways. Each pixel is one step (50 km or
    50 m/s). Pointer capture keeps the drag going even if the mouse leaves
    the number. Arrow keys work when it's selected (Shift = 10 steps). I
    got the idea from Bret Victor's Tangle and from Red Blob Games.

  Clicking words (WordToggle in Lab.tsx). Clicking "the same density all
    the way through" or "we ignore air" switches the interior model or
    drag. They're <span role="button"> instead of <button> because Chrome
    won't let a button wrap onto two lines.

  Guess first (markGuess in Lab.tsx). You type a guess in seconds and it
    gets marked in red pen: within 5 % gets a tick, otherwise it says how
    far off you were. Guessing before you see the answer helps you
    remember it (from the NYT "You Draw It" articles).

  Linked graphs (timeline.ts, Charts.tsx, CrossSection.tsx).
    timeline.ts holds three numbers: time (where the animation is), hover
    (the time under your mouse on a graph) and end. It's not React state
    on purpose. It changes 60 times a second while the probe falls, and if
    it were state in Lab, all eight graphs would redraw every frame. So
    only the parts that need it listen to it:
      - CrossSection draws the probe at `time`, plus a dashed pencil
        "ghost" probe at `hover`. The readout follows your mouse.
      - Each time graph has three divs on top of it: a red "now" line, a
        dashed hover line, and a white cover that hides the part the probe
        hasn't reached yet, so the graph draws itself as it falls. They
        move with transform: translateX(...%), which is cheap and never
        makes Recharts redraw.
      - The g vs r graph uses followX to turn the time into the probe's
        distance from the centre, so a red line slides along r.
    The divs have to line up with Recharts' plot area, so Charts.tsx keeps
    the plot edges in one PLOT constant. If you change the chart margins,
    change PLOT too.

  Ghost of the last run (Lab.tsx). After you change a setting, the
    velocity graph keeps your last run as a dashed pencil line. useSettled
    waits until you've stopped dragging for 350 ms, so the ghost is the
    last setting you actually stopped on.

  Keyboard (CrossSection.tsx). Space drops or pauses, left/right arrows
    move 1 % through the fall (Shift = 10 %), R resets. These are ignored
    while you're typing or using a control.

  Animation. The only real animation is the yellow highlighter swiping
    across the answer when it changes (the <p> gets a new key so the CSS
    animation plays again). Everything else is a 120 ms colour or opacity
    change. If "reduce motion" is on in your system settings, the swipe
    is turned off.


10. COMMON CHANGES
-----------------
  Change a given value (radius, g0, population) .. top of physics.ts
  Change the probe size .......................... PROBE_RADIUS, physics.ts
  Change the time step ........................... DT, physics.ts
  Change the dt values in the results table ...... STEP_SIZES, Lab.tsx
  Change slider ranges ........................... the <input type="range">
                                                   lines in Lab.tsx
  Change colours ................................. :root in globals.css AND
                                                   theme.ts
  Add a graph .................................... another <Chart ... /> in
                                                   Lab.tsx
